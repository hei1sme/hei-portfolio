## Overview

SmokeNet is a transformer-based forecasting system that I designed after months of air-quality monitoring in Melbourne.  
The aim was to **predict PM2.5 levels up to 24 hours ahead** and translate raw concentrations into practical health alerts.

## Key Outcomes

- **Published at AJCAI 2025** with a research paper detailing the end-to-end system.
- Achieved **MAE 0.7470** and **R^2 0.9545** on the held-out 2024 evaluation split (35.9% MAE reduction vs an XGBoost baseline).
- In the **May 2024 smoke-event backtest**, MAE was **57.7% lower than the baseline**. This is a separate evaluation from the held-out result above.

## Architecture

- Custom transformer encoder with seasonal embeddings and a reconstruction loss that stabilises long-horizon error.
- Chronological cross-validation keeps training and evaluation ordered in time.
- GPU-accelerated training on rolling 2020-2025 data ingested from the OpenWeather API.

## Explainability

Transparency is central to the project:

- Integrated **SHAP**, **Integrated Gradients**, and **DiCE** to audit forecasts both globally and per prediction.
- Generated clinic-friendly rationales that highlight how humidity shifts, wind direction, or burn-off events influence alerts.

## Product Surface

- Dashboard prototype that summarises next-day PM2.5 levels, confidence bands, and response playbooks.
- Health guidance tiers mapped to WHO thresholds and local emergency procedures.

## Lessons

- The model comparison depends on the data split and evaluation protocol. I report the held-out and smoke-event results separately.
- Attribution and counterfactual analysis help inspect a forecast. They do not establish that the system is ready for public-safety use.
