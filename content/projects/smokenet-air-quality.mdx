## Overview

SmokeNet is a transformer-based forecasting study using Melbourne air-quality data from the OpenWeather API. It uses **48 hours of history to predict next-day mean PM2.5**, then connects forecasts to an interpretable alerting prototype.

## Publication and evaluation

I am the **first and corresponding author** of the paper published at **AJCAI 2025, Springer LNAI**. The [published chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-4969-6_34) contains the numerical results and their experimental setup.

Training and validation precede a **held-out 2024 test year**. The May 2024 smoke-event backtest is a separate evaluation, not an extra independent test year. Metrics in the paper are reported on the original PM2.5 concentration scale after inverse transforms.

## Architecture

- Custom transformer encoder with seasonal embeddings and a reconstruction loss that stabilises long-horizon error.
- Chronological cross-validation keeps training and evaluation ordered in time.
- Data from the OpenWeather Air Pollution API, spanning late 2020 to early 2025.

## Explainability

Transparency is central to the project:

- Integrated **SHAP**, **Integrated Gradients**, and **DiCE** to audit forecasts both globally and per prediction.
- Used attribution and counterfactual analysis to inspect forecast drivers and possible changes to model inputs.

## Product Surface

- Dashboard prototype for next-day PM2.5 forecasts and explanations.
- Proposed alert tiers mapped to the U.S. EPA AQI categories documented in the paper.

## Lessons

- The model comparison depends on the data split and evaluation protocol. I report the held-out and smoke-event results separately.
- The original evaluation logs are not linked here. A consistency check on the paper's baseline table is pending; its numbers should not be treated as an independently reproduced benchmark.
- Attribution and counterfactual analysis help inspect a forecast. They do not establish that the system is ready for public-safety use.
